    01  How it works

    It reads for shape, not for truth.

    Persuasion leans on familiar wording. Borrowed agreement. Experts with no names. A deadline. A doubter made to look foolish. BiasClear looks for that wording, outlines each match, and tells you what the move is called.

    It does not decide whether a claim is right. It does not score the text or the writer. It shows the build, and leaves the reading to you.

    1. You paste text. It never leaves this tab.
    2. The {{generalCount}} general rules in the rule pack look for known wordings. A claim with a citation right beside it can be left unmarked.
    3. Each move is outlined, named, and sorted into one of three tiers.
    4. You decide what it means. Nothing is scored.

    Tier I

    Ideological

    What you are told is settled. These moves make a claim feel agreed, proven or decided before you have weighed it, set unequal sides as even, or answer a worry with a promise.

    Tier II

    Psychological

    How you are pushed. These moves put fear, shame, pride, decency or a deadline where a reason should be, cut the choices to two or the causes to one, or wave off the doubter.

    Tier III

    Institutional

    Whose voice it borrows, and how large it makes things. These moves lean on credentials, unnamed bodies, findings given without reasons and process language to sound official, or call a harm total and final.

    How often the rules are right on new text: not measured yet. Read the marks as prompts to look closer, not as findings.

    03  Privacy

    Your text stays in this tab.

    No uploads.

    The rules run inside this page. What you paste is read here and sent nowhere.

    No cookies.

    The page sets none and stores nothing in your browser. It forgets your text when you close the tab.

    No accounts.

    Nothing to sign up for. Nothing to sign in to. Nothing to hand over.

    Check it yourself.

    The page, its scripts, its images and its two typefaces all come from this site. Once they are in, the page’s own code asks for nothing more, and its security policy lets it load files only from this site and blocks the usual ways a script sends data (connect-src 'none'). The counter under the text starts once the typefaces are loaded, then counts every request your browser reports. Paste something and watch it stay at 0, or look at this page’s requests in your browser’s network panel. Browser extensions can make requests of their own; the counter may show some of those too.

    Letters the typefaces do not include are drawn in your device’s own fonts. The privacy page has the rest, including what the host keeps.
