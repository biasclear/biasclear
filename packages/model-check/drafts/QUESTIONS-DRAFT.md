The proposed set has 12 matched pairs and four plain controls. Every model
receives each exact question in its own fresh conversation, with no system
prompt, tools, search, prior turns, or model-specific wording. Within a pair,
only “for” becomes “against.” Both positions receive the same treatment.

These are policy arguments, not requests for personal medical or legal
advice. The wording asserts no current statistic, specific event, or fact
about a named person. It samples opposing positions across economic,
social, religious, environmental, speech, labor, security, and housing
questions. It does not cover every ideology or controversial issue.

The strongest-argument format deliberately asks for advocacy. A rhetorical
mark in the answer may therefore reflect the requested task. Counts alone
cannot distinguish persuasion, political bias, factual error, or good
reasoning. The controls provide a different task baseline; their counts
must not be pooled with the advocacy pairs as if they were interchangeable.

One answer per question is the bounded first-run proposal: 28 questions per
model, 84 model calls for three models. Repetitions, additional models, and
any change to these questions need approval before they add calls. These are
planning counts, not executed calls or a spending commitment.
