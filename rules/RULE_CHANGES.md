# Rule changes since v1

This file records every way the v2 pack (`biasclear-rules.json`) differs from v1's 42 deterministic rules, and why.

The examples below are test inputs, each paired with its mirror, and some quote insults. The people, parties, organizations, faith bodies, outlets, schools, awards and programs in them are made up; countries, places, peoples and faiths are named as they are. Lists of words that earlier rule versions held are quoted as they were. No rule names anyone.

- **Rules version 2.0.0a1** carried 39 v1 rules unchanged and left out three whose regexes held lists of proper nouns.
- **Rules version 2.0.0a2** (ticket E3) replaces those three with structural rules, changed what 24 of the 39 rules it kept could mark (counted 2026-09-28; 23 through their patterns, and `TOTALIZING_HARM_LANGUAGE` through the citation fixes alone) so that names are read more alike, fixes citation look-alikes that silenced rules, and rewrites names and descriptions that stated a motive. It has 43 rules. The red team's 220 asymmetric pairs on the first E3 draft, the 301 of its second round, the 243 of its third, the 183 of its fourth and the 350 of its fifth were worked through in the same version, and every one is kept: most run as required checks, and the rest are known limits or retired pairs, each with its reason (see "Red-team pairs" and "Red team, second round" to "Red team, fifth round" below).
- **Rules version 2.0.0a3** (the site review) makes the citation patterns and `CREDENTIAL_AS_PREMISE` read long runs of name characters in linear time, adds a mirror the red team's review of the site found missing, takes a real organization out of a description, and takes out the words that named, or were built from the name of, a party, movement, ideology, faith, country or program, so that the rules catch each move by its structure for any group (the neutrality lint checks the pack against its reference list of such words) (see "Rules version 2.0.0a3" and "Rules hold no group names" below). It then treats the two parties' abbreviations alike, puts faith-neutral words in place of each faith's own words for its places and offices, adds a common-word insult where one side's had no counterpart, and retires the red-team pairs that changed the kind of word rather than the side (see "Same rules for every side").
- **Rules version 2.0.0a4** (the red team's first fix round on the public seed) makes the open slots take any word shape, so a people's, faith's or outlet's name is read the same whatever its ending; gives every closed list its mirrors; takes one country's statute and rule number out of `LEGAL_SANCTIONS_THREAT`; reads citations of every jurisdiction; leaves ordinary sentences alone in four frames; reads two patterns in linear time; and teaches the neutrality lint to see disguised words (see "Rules version 2.0.0a4" below). With it, the pack changes what 25 of the 39 rules it kept can mark.

The rule behind all of it is "Neutrality" in `AGENTS.md`: rules match structure, never named people, parties, outlets, institutions, schools or ideologies. What the tests show for rules 2.0.0a4, as `scripts/site_facts.py` counts it: 9,793 swapped pairs raise the same rules on both sides (8,313 template pairs and 1,480 red-team pairs), 20 known limits are listed with their reasons (below), and name slots and gaps hold up to 16 tokens, so a longer name can still stop a match. That is what "names are read more alike" means here, and no more.

## Rules version 2.0.0a5 (preview review)

`INSTITUTIONAL_POSITION_AS_SETTLED` checks for a lowercase continuation after all whitespace following a sentence-ending period or exclamation mark. Previously its sentence start checked only the next character, while its body skipped the whole whitespace run. Repeating `a.  a ` therefore started a new search at every period and rescanned the remaining input. The correction makes both checks treat repeated and mixed whitespace alike.

The Python and TypeScript regression fixtures include repeated spaces, tab-plus-space and nonbreaking-space-plus-space. Semantic controls retain uppercase assertions, lowercase continuations, abbreviations and evidence-backed exceptions. The existing 693 golden cases have unchanged text and expected moves; only their rules-version metadata changes. The Method page and all six affected Field Guide entries also disclose the inherited first-occurrence citation limit; citation behavior is unchanged.

## Left out of 2.0.0a1

v1 had 42 deterministic rules. 2.0.0a1 left out every rule whose regexes contain an alternation of two or more proper nouns, because a rule like that treats the same sentence differently depending on which name is in it. | | | "A <listed prize>-winning reporter wrote the story." | "A <prize not on the list>-winning reporter wrote the story." | |

## E3: rules version 2.0.0a2

### How windows work now

Several v1 rules allowed only a few words or characters between two parts of a phrase, so a short name fit and a long one did not ("because of the Varnholt Foundation everything ..." was flagged; "because of the Center for Ostrevan Progress everything ..." was not). E3 counts every such window in words, and uses one definition of a word wherever a name can sit:

- **Name token**: any run of characters other than spaces, commas, colons, semicolons, quotes, brackets and sentence ends. A period, "!", "?" or (since the fifth round) a colon may sit inside a token ("RallyOn.org's", "V.O.P.'s", "TJW Jr.'s", "Stó:lō"), and a token may end in an abbreviation period after a capital letter ("Dr.", "Sen.", "St.", "Msgr.", "W.", "Ph.D.") or in "!" before a lowercase word ("Zowie! endorses"). Curly apostrophes and en dashes count like their ASCII forms ("O’Callan", "Rennick–Thorley"). So a name never breaks a slot because of how it is punctuated, and a slot still stops at the end of a sentence or clause. Since the fifth round a slot token may also be a parenthesized word ("Berwick (Minn.)", "Maumee (Ohio)'s") and may end in a comma that does not start a clause ("Parents, Families and Allies of Lesbians and Gays"; not a comma before "and", "but", "which" and the like).
- **Slot**: up to 16 name tokens where a name or modifier sits inside a phrase ("all ... experts", "the ... consensus", "as a ... , I know"), 8 until the fifth round, whose longest official names ("the National Association for the Advancement of Tessalian People", "the Tessalian Federation of Labor and Congress of Industrial Organizations") ran past it. Two slots stay shorter: eight modifiers between a lowercase "leading", "top" or "key" and its group noun (a connector inside a name counts with the word before it, "Center for Ostrevan Progress scholars"), and four words after "every".
- **Gap**: up to 16 words between two parts of a rule ("because of ... everything", "some say ... while most economists ...", "leading ... says", "leading organizations ... agree", "the vast majority of ... agree"); "either ... or" allows up to 32 words of one sentence.
- **Lead**: `MEDIA_EMOTIONAL_LEAD` looks at the whole first sentence, however long, instead of a word count.

Every name in `tests/test_symmetry.py` fits these windows, including the longest official names the red team found. A longer name can still stop a match; that limit is the same for every name. `tests/test_doc_counts.py` checks the 16 and the 32 on the rules themselves.

### Names that contain a rule's words

The rules were case-insensitive and could not tell where a name ends, so "The Center for Ostrevan Progress has shown that ..." raised `INEVITABILITY_FRAME` on "Progress has shown", and "The Consensus Quarterly survey" raised `CONSENSUS_AS_EVIDENCE`. Five rules now use capital letters to tell a name from a common word:

- A trigger word ("history", "progress", "time", "the future", "research", "studies", "experts", "science", "data", "evidence") counts in lowercase anywhere, and capitalized or in capitals only at the start of the text or a sentence, not right after another word, where it would be the end of a name. "Brelland Research shows" now reads like "Kessop shows"; "Research shows" at the start of a sentence still counts.
- "consensus" in "the ... consensus" must be lowercase or in capitals, so a name that begins "Consensus" does not count.
- A capitalized "Consensus" or "Experts" still counts when no capitalized word follows it, so "the Tessaly Consensus is clear" reads like "the Tessaly consensus is clear", while "the Consensus Quarterly survey" and "the Experts Council" do not count. "experts" counts in any case in `CLAIM_WITHOUT_CITATION` ("All Carverton Experts agree" reads like "All experts agree").
- 19 rules have the flags `s` instead of `is`: the four above, `INSTITUTIONAL_POSITION_AS_SETTLED`, the ten that read names with the name token (whose abbreviation period depends on capitals: `CREDENTIAL_AS_PREMISE`, `MONOCAUSAL_BLAME`, `SOFT_CONSENSUS`, `COMPETENCE_DISMISSAL`, `VAGUE_INSTITUTIONAL_APPEAL`, `MEDIA_EDITORIAL_AS_NEWS`, `MEDIA_EMOTIONAL_LEAD`, `FALSE_BINARY`, `FIN_SURVIVORSHIP_BIAS`, and, since the fifth round, `MEDIA_WEASEL_QUANTIFIERS`), and, since the second round, `DISSENT_DISMISSAL`, `FEAR_URGENCY`, `MORAL_HIGH_GROUND` and `CAUSAL_TOTALIZATION`. Every other letter is written as a class like `[Ee]`, so the rest of each phrase matches in any case, including all capitals, as before. `tests/test_regex_subset.py` lists them (`CASE_SENSITIVE_RULES`).

"Before" is v1 (rules 2.0.0a1). "Now" is rules 2.0.0a4, after the red team's five rounds, the site review and the first fix round; the sections below say which round changed what.

### Bounded windows reviewed and kept

- `MEDIA_BURIED_QUALIFIER` keeps v1's `(?<=.{200})` and `.{5,150}?`. The rule is about where the qualifier sits in the text, so a position in characters is its subject. A long name earlier in the text can move a qualifier past the mark; 150 characters (about 25 words) holds any realistic name between "however" and the denial. The symmetry suite puts names of up to eight words there.
- `MEDIA_SELECTIVE_QUOTATION` bounds a quote to 2 to 40 characters, so that a quoted sentence is less likely to be read as a scare quote. The same bound applies to every label.
- `BUREAUCRATIC_OBSCURITY`'s one-word slot (`\s+\w+\s+` between jargon and "framework") and `FIN_ANCHORING`'s "a fraction" (`a\s+\w+`) hold a common word, not a name.
- `CREDENTIAL_AS_PREMISE` allows one to 16 name tokens between a status word and "says" ("a respected Senator Catherine Ortega Marco says", "renowned scholar Martin Lowell Kade Jr. says"), and, since the fifth round, up to 16 in "as a ..., I know", after "-educated" and after "laureate". An earlier draft allowed three, which a name of two or three words already filled.
- `INSTITUTIONAL_POSITION_AS_SETTLED` reads only the word before "has/have", so the rest of the subject can be any length.

### Word lists reviewed

The first E3 draft mirrored `DISSENT_DISMISSAL` only and judged the other lists side-neutral. The red team showed that was wrong: noun and label lists decided which side a sentence was about ("the embattled company" but not "the embattled union", "industry groups" but not "labor groups", "destroying the economy" but not "destroying the planet", "deniers" but not "truthers"). Those lists now hold both sides' words, as listed in the table above. In rules version 2.0.0a2 that was not enough: `DISSENT_DISMISSAL`'s lists also held the names of ideologies, parties, movements and faiths and slur blends of them ("socialists", "atheists", "demonrats"), and `CAUSAL_TOTALIZATION`'s held named programs and systems ("medicare", "capitalism"), each beside its mirror. Rules version 2.0.0a3 took every such word out ("Rules hold no group names"); the lists that remain hold common words that can be aimed at anyone.

Speed in JavaScript engines. The fourth round's patterns were correct but slow in V8, the engine behind Chrome and Node: the ReDoS guard (`packages/engine/test/redos.test.ts`) timed `DISSENT_DISMISSAL` at 50 to 140 ms on its adversarial strings, over the 50 ms limit, and a scan of 200,000 emoji took seconds. - The TypeScript translation writes letters and digits as themselves instead of as `\u{...}` escapes, so `[Aa]` takes 4 characters instead of 15 and a class of accented capitals one character per letter. Most translated patterns shrink by half or more.

No two branches of `CONSENSUS_AS_EVIDENCE` can start at the same word, so it finds the same moves.

### Party and country abbreviations

The name-token rules share one closed list of abbreviations that do not end a sentence ("Sen.", "Rev.", "St.", "Univ.", "Calif."). It is sentence-splitting data: it decides only whether a period ends a sentence. "Rules hold no group names" took "Dem." off it with "Amer." and "Am.", and kept "Rep." as the title of a Representative. That read the two parties differently: "Rep. Leaders said ..." was one sentence and "Dem. Leaders said ..." was two. The parties' abbreviations are treated alike now. "Rep." and "Dem." both stay, and the neutrality lint allows exactly these two dotted forms, inside the list only; anywhere else, without the period or in another form ("Dems"), it still fails. The red team's "Rep." / "Dem." pairs pass again and run with the fifth round's.

"Amer." and "Am." are decided by the same test: what the rules do must not differ by side or by nationality. Keeping one country's abbreviation would mean keeping every country's ("Brit.", "Can.", "Ger.", "Mex."), and a list of countries is what no rule may hold. So no country's or nationality's abbreviation is on the list, and each ends a sentence alike: "the Amer. Guild Assn." reads like "the Brit. Guild Assn.". Two spellings are on the list for another reason, "Fr." (a title, Father) and "Ind." (a state, Indiana), so "the Fr. Guild Assn." (French) goes on where "the Brit. Guild Assn." ends. Those pairs are known limits (`abbreviation`).

## The neutrality lint

`tests/test_neutrality_lint.py` fails if any regex in the pack has an alternation with two or more proper-noun branches: a branch that starts with a word from a reference list of names of the forbidden kinds, or with an acronym that has no lowercase twin in the same alternation. It catches each of the three v1 lists above.

Since rules 2.0.0a3 it also fails if any rule holds, even once, a word from a second reference list (`GROUP_WORDS`): the names of parties, movements, ideologies, faiths, countries and programs, words built from a person's name ("-ist", "-ism" and "-ite" forms), outlets, agencies, schools and prizes, each in the plural and with "anti", "neo", "ultra" and "pro" in front, and slur blends of them ("demonrats", "magats", "libtards", "christofascists", "bible-thumpers", "America-haters", "Amer."). It reads each regex as the words it can spell literally: a case-flexed letter such as `[Ss]` reads as one letter, an optional letter or group both ways, a hyphen, space or period as a word break, and the pattern inside each lookaround on its own. Since rules 2.0.0a4 it also reads each regex the way someone hiding a word would write it ("The neutrality lint reads disguised words" above). It also reads each rule's name and description and each tier's text. A word from a longer or shorter family in the text ("history" beside "tory", "republic" beside "republican") does not count; only the whole word does.

A mutation check proves it: `test_group_word_lint_catches_a_group_word_added_to_the_pack` adds "socialists", "demonrats", "magats" and "bible-thumpers", one at a time, to `DISSENT_DISMISSAL`, and "medicare" to a description, and requires the lint to fail each time. The same was done by hand on the pack file for "socialists" and "demonrats" when the lint was written: the lint named the word and the indicator it sat in, and passed again once the word was taken out. The reference list cannot hold every name, so the swapped pairs check the rules too.

The reference lists are denylists: they hold the names and word forms no rule may hold, including ideology, faith, party and eponym forms built from real people's names ("marxist", "lutheran", and forms built from living politicians' names), because the lint exists to keep them out. They are kept in plain text, not hashed, so anyone can read what is checked. The lint file is the only place in the repository where forms built from a living person's name may appear (a test checks every other file), and it is excluded by path from the checks that keep real people's names out of the repository. Since the faith-neutral change the list also holds each faith's own words for its places and offices ("churches", "mosques", "rabbis").

One exception, and only one: "Rep." and "Dem." may stand inside the closed abbreviation list, beside the titles of every major faith, the sentence-splitting data shared by the name-token rules ("Party and country abbreviations"). The lint takes exactly those two dotted forms out of that list before it reads a pattern. Mutation tests check that the exception is exact: "Dem." or "Dems" anywhere else, "Amer.", "Am." or "Gop" inside the list, and the list followed by anything but a period all fail. Another test checks that every copy of the list is the same list, holds both parties' abbreviations and no country's, and holds every major faith's titles.

Calendar words are allowed. Months and weekdays are closed-class calendar terms, not entities: they carry no political or institutional side. The lint holds them in an explicit allowlist (`CALENDAR_TERMS`), and a test checks that `FIN_CHERRY_PICKED_TIMEFRAME` passes only because of it.
